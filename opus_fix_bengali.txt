============================================================
8. INT8 EXPERIMENT — FAILED FOR SPEED
============================================================

ONNX Runtime dynamic INT8 quantization was tested.

Two variants:

1. MatMul-only:
- 114,404,484 bytes
- effectively no useful size reduction
- not tested further on phone

2. MatMul + Conv:
- 38,067,490 bytes
- desktop synthesis worked
- Android synthesis worked
- no obvious output corruption
- much slower than fp32

Phone comparison, same sentences/order, 4 threads:

FP32:
- model: 114,065,046 B
- APK: 608,240,873 B
- init: 4.1 s
- cold synthesis: 8.1 s
- cold audible start: 14.1 s
- warm median RTF: 2.46
- warm start: 6.9 s
- Bengali PSS: 299,622 KB
- all voices PSS: 728,244 KB

INT8 MatMul+Conv:
- model: 38,067,490 B
- APK: 531,711,369 B
- init: 5.4 s
- cold synthesis: 21.7 s
- cold audible start: 27.9 s
- warm median RTF: 6.7
- RTF range: 5.97–8.10
- warm median start: 19.8 s
- max start: 26.6 s
- Bengali PSS: 240,487 KB
- all voices PSS: 681,050 KB

Stability:
- 22 runs
- 0 errors
- text integrity preserved

Conclusion:
Dynamic INT8 clearly failed as a performance optimization.
It only helped size and memory.

Working hypothesis from the previous agent:
dynamic quantization converted many convolutions to ConvInteger and ONNX Runtime may be hitting slow kernels on both x86 and ARM.

This was NOT profiled and must be treated as a hypothesis, not fact.

Do not repeat the same dynamic INT8 experiment unless profiling gives a concrete reason.
